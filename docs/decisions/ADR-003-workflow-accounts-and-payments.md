# ADR-003: the workflow is accounts and payments

- **Status:** accepted, recorded 2026-09-29. It closes the choice `docs/plan.md` (2026-09-28) left open between
  payments and complaints, and it replaces that plan's reading of balance queries (see "What we gave up").
- **Context:** the brief asks for one focused, coherent workflow, chosen with the supplied data, with a normal path, an
  ambiguous or unsupported request and a case for a person. More workflows earn no bonus.

## Decision

The assistant answers **account and payment inquiries** for a retail bank in Mexico, Colombia and Argentina, in Spanish
and Portuguese: balances, recent transactions, the state of a payment and any arrears, exchange rates, and one action
(ADR-002). Everything else is abstained on or handed to a person.

Why this one, from the contact-center data (`docs/evidence/baseline_metrics.md`, regenerated by `make analysis`):

- It is the largest contact reason: **35.0%** of 686 thousand contacts.
- It costs the customer a wait: 120 s in the queue for a 221 s call, and the customers still rate it 2.91 out of 5.
- It is already resolvable (91.5% first-contact resolution), so automation is measured against a real human baseline
  of about 341 s per inquiry, not against a hard problem people also fail at.
- It has a verifiable ground truth: balances, transactions and payment states are in the warehouse, so a reply is right
  or wrong against a record and not against an opinion.

## Trade-offs, and what we gave up

- **The text of the transcripts is not evidence for this choice.** The 171 thousand customer transcripts hold 42
  distinct customer texts, the text does not vary with the contact reason, and there is no Portuguese
  (`docs/data_quality.md`). The choice rests on **volume, waiting time and resolution by reason**, which are counted
  per contact, and not on what customers wrote. For the same reason every utterance we train or evaluate on is
  team-written, which `LIMITATIONS.md` states.
- The plan of 2026-09-28 set balance queries aside because the transcripts repeat two opening questions and so do not
  prove demand for them. That reading confused the *text* with the *demand*: the count of contacts by reason is
  independent of the repeated text, and it puts this reason first.
- Other workflows (card support, disputes, credit eligibility) are not attempted. A dispute workflow would have a
  stronger business story per case; it would also need an action with money consequences and a policy we could only
  invent.

## Consequences

- The one action stays small (ADR-002) and reversible for the customer: a trace request, never a money movement.
- Credit and eligibility are out of scope, so the brief's separation of risk estimates and eligibility policy does not
  apply, and no label of the dataset drives an automated decision (ADR-005).
