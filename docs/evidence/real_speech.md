## 6. Reading it (written after the results were seen)

The keyword baseline answers `out_of_scope` whenever no keyword matches, and 62.5% [57.6–67.2] (n=384) of the primary calls are `out_of_scope`, so most of its accuracy is that default, not skill. Two cuts put the result in proportion:

- **Only the in-scope calls** (`balance`, `latest_transactions`): learned 89.6% [83.5–93.6] (n=144), keyword 81.9% [74.9–87.4] (n=144); the learned classifier is right where the keywords are wrong on 14 and the reverse on 3 (McNemar p = 0.012726). Where the assistant has a tool to run, the learned reading does at least as well on real speech.
- **What happens to an out-of-scope call** (the weak spot): read correctly 63.7% [57.5–69.6] (n=240); sent to a person 24.6% [19.6–30.4] (n=240); read as an in-scope request 11.7% [8.2–16.3] (n=240). The first error is a wasted hand-off, the second a wrong tool the policy and the confirmation step still have to catch.

Both numbers are about one model and one dataset, and the data is now spent as an external test: changing the classifier and re-scoring on the same calls would not be a test.

## What this does not show

- European Spanish and Portuguese, transcribed by ASR, against a model trained on Latin American text typed by the team: a real distribution shift, but not our customers.
- The labels are the dataset's own, mapped to ours by a rule written beforehand. No second person re-annotated them.
- The dataset has no `payment_status` or `exchange_rate_inquiry` calls, so two of our six classes are untested here.
- Calls whose dataset intent is `freeze` or `card_issues` are excluded, so this says nothing about whether real fraud and theft calls are escalated.
